The problem

The bottleneck moved from drafting to verification.

AI made drafting fast, but checking the answer became more time-consuming than writing it. A fluent response still has to be verified line by line, and by hand that is often slower than not using AI at all — so teams either skip the check and carry the risk, or skip the AI and lose the speed.

The failure mode that erodes trust is not the obvious error; it is the confident, well-formed answer that is quietly wrong and gets used because no one caught it. Ground Truth performs the per-claim check automatically, so verification runs at generation speed rather than manual-review speed.

“A model that reports an error is more valuable than one that silently fails or provides incorrect information.”
— First Army, evaluating a fielded AI assistant
What it is

A verification layer that runs after any model

Ground Truth is a model-agnostic verification layer. Every claim an AI emits is checked against a human-approved ledger of your organization's own authoritative sources. Supported claims pass and carry their citation; claims that conflict with an approved line are flagged and corrected from that source; anything ungrounded is flagged or refused. It is model-independent — no retraining — and attaches a portable verification record to every output.

What it is

  • A check on what the model said, after it says it
  • Traceability to your approved authority — doctrine, policy, orders, today's report
  • A deterministic gate — no model grading the model
  • Model-independent — the same check in front of any model

What it is not

  • Not a better model, and not a way to make a weak model accurate
  • Not RAG — RAG feeds a model context; this audits what it said
  • Not a content-safety filter scoring toxicity or policy categories
  • Not a fact-checker of the world — it checks traceability, not truth
RAG retrieves context.  Ground Truth resolves authority.
How it works

From approved source to verified output

Four steps, one engine. Authority comes from the source and the human who approved it; the machine only determines — deterministically — whether each claim is faithful to that approved source.

A worked example — carried through the rest of this briefing

During planning, an operator asks the unit's AI assistant to summarize the movement plan and engagement authorities for tonight's mission. The answer contains four statements. Two trace cleanly to the mission's approved sources. One is outside those sources entirely. And one — “ROE permits engagement without PID” — is fluent, plausible, and contradicts the approved ROE annex. Relationship to adjacent approaches

Ground Truth occupies a specific position among the techniques it is most often compared to. Each addresses a different question; the first two are complementary and commonly run alongside it.

ApproachWhat it doesWhat it does not provide
RAG Retrieves relevant text so the model can produce a better answer. Optimizes for recall. No check on what the model actually said. Retrieval is not approval — a chunk in an index carries no authority.
Probabilistic hallucination detectors (groundedness scorers, LLM-as-judge) Score how consistent an output is with retrieved context. Useful as monitoring signal. A model grading a model — probabilistic, no approval chain, no provenance, no portable record.
Policy-as-logic guardrails (e.g., AWS Bedrock Automated Reasoning) Deterministically check output against policies hand-translated into formal logic rules. Rules are encoded by hand, not extracted from source documents; cloud- and platform-bound; no source provenance chain.
Ground Truth Deterministically checks each claim for attribution to your own approved sources, with a human approval gate and a signed per-output verification record. Does not judge truth about the world, and does not improve the model — it bounds and documents what the model's output rests on.
Design properties

Conservative by construction

The governing constraint is an asymmetry: the system may fail to flag a claim, but it must never vouch for one it cannot trace. Nothing is certified without a deterministic match or a human decision; probabilistic components only propose candidates for review — they never certify.

The system can fail to flag. It must never falsely certify.
Deterministic

Not AI grading AI

The citation check and the pass/flag/block gate are code, not a model. AI saves human labor; it never sets ground truth. The model can't talk its way past a rule.

Honest boundary

Attribution, not truth

It proves a claim is — or isn't — traceable to approved authority. It never claims to know the world. That boundary is the source of its credibility.

The human gate

People approve; models propose

An entry is citable only after a designated reviewer confirms it is faithful to the source. Unverified stays visibly unverified.

Model-independent

Any model, no retraining

The same ledger and verifier sit in front of ChatGPT, an EdgeRunner SLM, or anything OpenAI- or Ollama-compatible. Swap the model; keep the guarantee.

Standards-aligned

Built to ICD 203

Every output links back to verifiable evidence — or is flagged when it can't — consistent with IC analytic-tradecraft standards for sourcing and analytic rigor.

Compounding asset

Compounds with use

Every check is data: gaps become proposed entries, repeated conflicts trigger doctrine review. The approved ledger becomes a versioned institutional asset that outlives any single model.

Policy alignment

The DoD AI Ethical Principles require AI to be traceable and governable; CDAO's generative-AI guidance requires human accountability and no silent failure. Ground Truth is a concrete implementation of those requirements — per-claim traceability to approved sources, a human approval gate, and visible refusal rather than silent error. The compliance argument is not "trust the vendor"; it is an implementation of the policy the Department has already written.

Enforcement modes

How strictly the sources govern the output

Three enforcement levels. The first changes nothing about how users work and only adds visibility; each subsequent level gives the approved sources more control over the output, as the stakes require.

Level 1 · Visibility

Annotate

The model answers freely; every statement is marked supported, needs-review, or unsupported. Full visibility, no interference. The default — no change to existing workflows.

In the example — the ROE claim is visibly marked as conflicting with the approved annex. Nothing is altered; the operator sees the conflict before acting.

Level 2 · Correction

Correct from source

Statements that conflict with an approved source are flagged and corrected — with the source shown, so the change is visible and attributable. For drafting against doctrine, orders, or specifications.

In the example — the ROE claim is replaced with the approved ROE line, citation attached, and the substitution is shown rather than silent.

Level 3 · Assurance

Constrain to sources

The model may state only what an approved entry supports, and must cite it. Anything else is refused rather than approximated. For the highest-stakes work.

In the example — the answer is limited to the claims the sources support: the LD time and CASEVAC plan pass with citations; the ROE and MSR statements are refused outright.

Findings surface however the workflow requires — annotated inline, logged silently, or blocking release.

The verification record

Every output carries a verification record

The record shown here is the one the worked example produces — the four claims, their verdicts, and the approved sources behind them. Every session produces one. It is called a WBOM — a Warrant Bill of Materials, deliberately modeled on the SBOM accreditors already use — and it records each statement, the approved source it traces to, what was flagged or refused, and who approved each source.

It is hash-bound to the exact source set checked, and signable, so later alteration is detectable. It is the artifact provided to any reviewer, approving authority, or accreditor who needs to confirm where a given claim originated — without repeating the verification by hand.

The format is open by design. The WBOM is a machine-readable record, not a proprietary report: it can be stored, compared, and checked by your own tooling, and reading one requires no EdgeRunner software. It is designed to align with the bill-of-materials formats federal accreditation pipelines already consume (the SBOM family of standards), so it enters existing review processes rather than demanding a new one.
